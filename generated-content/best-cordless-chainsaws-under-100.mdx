## What to Look for Before Buying the Best Cordless Chainsaws Under 100

### Find Your Need - Compatibility and use case considerations
Before you purchase, determine the size of the wood you typically cut. If you are only trimming small branches or shrubs, a 6-inch mini saw is perfectly adequate and easier to maneuver. However, if you need to clear fallen trees or thick logs, look for 12 or 16 inch models. Compatibility with your existing tools might also matter if you already own batteries from the same brand, though most of these kits include everything you need.

### Budget - Setting realistic price expectations ($50-$100 ranges)
In the sub-100 range, you can expect a wide variety of quality levels. The lower end ($50-$70) usually features 6-inch mini saws with brushed motors. As you move toward the 100 mark, you start seeing brushless motors, automatic oiling systems, and larger 4.0Ah batteries. Investing closer to 100 often yields a tool that lasts significantly longer and handles tougher tasks.

### Key Features - Most important technical specifications
Focus on the motor type and chain speed. Brushless motors are more durable and efficient. Chain speed, measured in ft/s, determines how quickly the saw will bite into the wood. Other critical features include automatic oilers, which keep the tool running smoothly, and tool-free tensioning, which saves you from carrying extra wrenches in the yard.

### Quality & Durability - What to look for in construction
Check for materials like manganese steel for the chain and high-impact plastic for the body. A good saw should feel solid in your hand without excessive rattling. Look for copper motors rather than aluminum-wound ones, as copper handles heat better and lasts longer under load.

### Brand Reputation - Importance of established manufacturers
While many brands in this price bracket are newer, look for those with a history of positive customer service and warranty support. Brands that offer at least a 1-year warranty show confidence in their product. Reviews that mention responsive customer support are a good sign that the company stands by its manufacturing quality.

### Portability/Size - Considerations for space and storage
Consider where you will store the tool. Many kits come with carrying cases, which are excellent for keeping your chains, batteries, and charger organized. If you have limited shed space, a compact 6-inch model is ideal. If you have a large property, a lightweight model under 4 lbs will be much easier to carry to the far corners of your yard.

## Is it A Wise Decision to Buy cordless chainsaws under 100?

Investing in a cordless chainsaw under 100 is a smart move for most residential homeowners. These tools have reached a point where battery technology and motor efficiency rival older, more expensive gas models for light to medium tasks. You save money not only on the initial purchase but also on fuel, spark plugs, and air filters. 

Compared to gas alternatives, these electric saws are quieter, lighter, and start instantly with the pull of a trigger. While they may not be suitable for professional loggers who need to cut wood for 8 hours a day, they are perfect for the occasional storm cleanup, seasonal pruning, or firewood prep. The long-term benefit of a cleaner, quieter, and more affordable tool makes it a highly justifiable investment for anyone with a garden or backyard.

## What Is the Best Way to Choose cordless chainsaws under 100?

Selecting the right saw requires a step-by-step approach to ensure you don't end up with a tool that is either underpowered or too bulky for your needs.

1. **Assess the Workload:** Walk your property and measure the diameter of the branches you need to cut. A good rule of thumb is to choose a bar length at least 2 inches longer than the thickest branch.
2. **Prioritize Safety:** Always look for a dual-activation switch. Some models also offer chain brakes which are a huge safety bonus.
3. **Check the Battery Capacity:** Look for kits that include two batteries. This allows you to charge one while using the other, preventing work stoppages.
4. **Evaluate Maintenance Needs:** If you don't want to carry an oil bottle, choose a model with an automatic oiling system.
5. **Read User Feedback:** Focus on reviews from people who have used the tool for several months to see how the motor holds up over time.

Avoid the mistake of buying the cheapest possible option without checking the motor type; brushless motors are worth the extra few dollars. If you are a beginner, stick with a 6-inch model until you get comfortable with the cutting action.
