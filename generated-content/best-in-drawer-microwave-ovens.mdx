## What to Look for Before Buying the Best In Drawer Microwave Ovens

Choosing a drawer microwave is a significant investment that requires more planning than a standard countertop unit. Since these are built-in appliances, they need to fit your kitchen layout perfectly while providing the functionality you expect from a modern microwave.

### 1. Find Your Need - Compatibility and use case considerations
The first step is determining where the unit will go. Are you installing it in a kitchen island, a base cabinet, or a wall unit? Most drawer microwaves are designed for under-counter installation, which is ideal for accessibility. Consider who will be using it; drawer models are great for children and individuals with mobility issues because there is no reaching up to high shelves.

### 2. Budget - Setting realistic price expectations ($X-$Y ranges)
Drawer microwaves are more expensive than countertop models. Generally, you should expect to spend between $800 and $1,500.
- **$800 - $1,000:** Standard 24-inch models with basic touch controls and 950-1000W power.
- **$1,000 - $1,300:** Models with hidden or angled control panels, sensor cooking, and premium finishes.
- **$1,300+:** 30-inch units or designer finishes like black stainless or matte black.

### 3. Key Features - Most important technical specifications
Look for wattage (950W-1000W is standard), capacity (usually 1.2 cu. ft.), and control type. Sensor cooking is a major plus as it prevents overcooking. Also, check for an automatic opening and closing system, as this adds a layer of luxury and convenience to the daily experience.

### 4. Quality & Durability - What to look for in construction
Since the drawer moves on a rail system, the quality of the glides is paramount. Stainless steel interiors are preferred for their durability and ease of cleaning. Avoid units that feel flimsy when the drawer is extended. Brands like Sharp and KoolMore are known for their robust mechanical designs.

### 5. Brand Reputation - Importance of established manufacturers
Sharp actually holds many of the patents for microwave drawer technology and manufactures units for several other high-end brands. Choosing a brand with a strong USA-based support system, like COSMO or Sharp, ensures that you can get parts or service if something goes wrong after the warranty period.

### 6. Portability/Size - Considerations for space and storage
Drawer microwaves are not portable; they are permanent fixtures. You must ensure you have the correct electrical outlet (usually a dedicated 120V circuit) and the exact dimensions for the cutout. Standard widths are 24 and 30 inches, but the depth and height can vary slightly between brands.

## Is it A Wise Decision to Buy in drawer microwave ovens?

Buying an in-drawer microwave oven is an excellent decision for anyone looking to maximize kitchen space and improve ergonomics. Traditional microwaves often take up a large portion of the countertop or are placed above the stove, which can be dangerous when handling hot liquids. By moving the microwave to a drawer, you reclaim that workspace and place the appliance at a height that is safer and easier for everyone in the family to use.

From a home value perspective, built-in appliances are often seen as a premium upgrade. They provide a custom, high-end look that can be a selling point for your home in the future. While the initial cost is higher than a standard microwave, the long-term benefits of improved kitchen flow and aesthetics often justify the investment. Furthermore, drawer microwaves are engineered specifically for built-in use, meaning they have proper venting systems that many countertop models lack when placed inside a cabinet.

## What Is the Best Way to Choose in drawer microwave ovens?

To choose the right model, follow this step-by-step guide:
1. **Measure Your Cabinets:** This is the most critical step. Ensure your cabinet width matches the microwave (24" or 30") and check the depth and height requirements.
2. **Determine Your Finish:** Match the microwave finish to your existing appliances (Stainless, Black Stainless, or Matte Black).
3. **Decide on Controls:** Do you want a hidden control panel for a sleeker look, or an angled panel for easier access?
4. **Evaluate Features:** Determine if you need sensor cooking or if standard presets are enough for your needs.
5. **Check the Warranty:** Look for a minimum of a 1-year warranty with good customer support.

Avoid the mistake of buying a 30-inch microwave just because you have a 30-inch cabinet if the interior capacity is the same as the 24-inch model; sometimes the extra width is just cosmetic and takes up more space than necessary. If you have a small kitchen, a 24-inch model is usually the best balance of size and performance.
