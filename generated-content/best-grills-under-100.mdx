## What to Look for Before Buying the Best Grills Under 100

### 1. Find Your Need - Compatibility and use case considerations
Before you purchase, determine if you need a primary cooking unit or if you are looking to protect your current setup. If you are buying a grill, consider the fuel type—propane is portable and easy to find, while natural gas requires a dedicated line but offers continuous fuel. For mats, match the dimensions of the mat to the footprint of your grill plus an extra six inches on all sides for splatter protection.

### 2. Budget - Setting realistic price expectations ($X-$Y ranges)
In the sub-$100 and entry-level category, you can find excellent tabletop grills and high-end protective mats. For full-sized standing grills, the $150-$300 range often offers the best balance of longevity and features. If you are strictly under $100, focus on high-quality accessories that enhance your existing cooking experience or look for seasonal clearance on compact 2-burner models.

### 3. Key Features - Most important technical specifications
Look for BTUs (British Thermal Units) which measure heat output; around 80-100 BTUs per square inch is a good baseline. Porcelain-coated cast iron grates are the gold standard for heat retention and easy cleaning. For mats, a heat rating of at least 500 degrees Fahrenheit is necessary for gas grills, while fiberglass options rated up to 2000 degrees are better for charcoal or wood-burning pits.

### 4. Quality & Durability - What to look for in construction
