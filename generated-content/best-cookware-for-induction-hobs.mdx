## What to Look for Before Buying the Best Cookware For Induction Hobs

### 1. Find Your Need - Compatibility and use case considerations
The first step is deciding between a full set of induction-ready cookware and adapter plates. If you have an expensive collection of copper or aluminum pans, an adapter plate is your best friend. However, if you are starting from scratch, a dedicated induction set like the BEZIA 10 Piece will offer better energy efficiency and faster heating times because the magnetic connection is direct.

### 2. Budget - Setting realistic price expectations ($15-$150 ranges)
Individual adapter plates typically range from $15 to $40 depending on the size and material quality. These are great for a tight budget. Full cookware sets can range from $80 for entry-level aluminum sets to over $300 for professional-grade stainless steel. Setting a budget early helps you decide if you want to replace everything at once or slowly transition using adapters.

### 3. Key Features - Most important technical specifications
Look for a triple-layer or multi-ply construction. This usually involves an aluminum core for heat distribution and stainless steel for durability and magnetic attraction. For adapter plates, the handle design is crucial—removable or foldable handles are superior for storage. For sets, look for non-stick coatings that are PFAS and PFOA free to ensure healthy cooking.

### 4. Quality & Durability - What to look for in construction
On induction, cheap pans can sometimes hum or buzz. To avoid this, look for cookware with a heavy, flat base. The weight usually indicates a thicker magnetic plate which provides a more stable connection and better heat retention. For stainless steel, the 304 grade is the gold standard for rust resistance and long-term shine.

## What Is the Best Way to Choose cookware for induction hobs?

The best way to choose is to perform a simple magnet test on your current pans. If a magnet sticks firmly to the bottom, the pan is induction-ready. For those that fail, measure the diameter of the pan base. This will tell you exactly what size adapter plate you need.

Start by identifying your most-used pan. If it is a small milk pan, start with a 5.5-inch adapter. If you find yourself using many different sizes, a set with removable handles is the most space-efficient upgrade. Always check the safety ratings of non-stick coatings and ensure that any adapter you buy has an insulated handle. Avoid buying overly cheap, single-layer plates, as they tend to warp under the intense heat of induction, which can damage your expensive glass cooktop.
