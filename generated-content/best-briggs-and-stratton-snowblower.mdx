## What to Look for Before Buying the Best Briggs And Stratton Snowblower

### Find Your Need - Compatibility and use case considerations
Before you make a purchase, it is vital to understand the scope of your snow removal needs. If you have a long driveway with frequent heavy snowfall, a 2-stage blower with a 208cc engine or higher is essential. However, if you are looking to maintain your existing equipment, you must match the specific model number of your engine to the parts you are buying. Briggs and Stratton engines have very specific series numbers like the 800 or 1150 series, and parts are rarely universal across all types.

### Budget - Setting realistic price expectations ($15-$800 ranges)
Setting a budget involves looking at both the machine and its upkeep. A full-sized PowerSmart snowblower with a Briggs engine will typically range from $600 to $900. On the maintenance side, carburetors and recoil starters usually cost between $20 and $50. It is often much more cost-effective to spend $30 on a repair kit than $700 on a new machine. We recommend setting aside a small annual budget of $50 for items like synthetic oil and fuel stabilizers to prevent larger expenses later.

### Key Features - Most important technical specifications
When evaluating a snowblower, look for an electric start feature. In cold climates, pull-starting an engine can be physically exhausting. Clearing width is another major factor; 24 inches is the sweet spot for most residential driveways. For parts, check for factory calibration. A carburetor that is pre-set will save you hours of frustration trying to find the right air-to-fuel ratio in the middle of a snowstorm.

### Quality & Durability - What to look for in construction
Durability in a snowblower comes down to the quality of the steel in the auger and the housing. For replacement parts, look for aluminum alloys and high-density polymers. Recoil ropes should be made of reinforced nylon to prevent snapping. If you are buying a fuel tank, ensure it is made of UV-resistant, anti-rust materials, as it will be exposed to moisture and cold for months at a time.

### Brand Reputation - Importance of established manufacturers
Briggs and Stratton is a household name for a reason. They have been manufacturing small engines for over a century. Choosing a machine with a Briggs engine means you have access to a massive network of service centers and a nearly endless supply of aftermarket parts. Even if you buy a third-party accessory, ensure it specifically mentions compatibility with OEM Briggs part numbers to guarantee a proper fit.

### Portability/Size - Considerations for space and storage
A 2-stage snowblower is a large piece of equipment. Before buying, measure your garage or shed to ensure you have space. Many models feature folding handles to help with storage. For those with limited space, keeping an older, smaller machine running with new carburetors and fresh synthetic oil might be a better strategy than buying a massive new unit that you cannot easily store.

## Is it A Wise Decision to Buy briggs and stratton snowblower?

Investing in a Briggs and Stratton powered snowblower is one of the smartest decisions a homeowner in a snowy region can make. These engines are specifically designed to operate in low temperatures, utilizing overhead valve technology that provides more power in a smaller footprint. Compared to electric alternatives, gas-powered Briggs engines offer unlimited runtime and the raw torque needed to throw heavy, wet slush that would often clog a battery-operated unit.

While the initial cost of a gas machine is higher, the long-term benefits are substantial. Because the parts are so widely available, these machines can easily last 15 to 20 years with basic maintenance. If a part fails, you can find a replacement on Amazon for a fraction of the cost of a new machine, as shown in our reviews. This makes it a much more sustainable and cost-effective investment over a decade compared to cheaper, disposable brands where parts are impossible to source.

## What Is the Best Way to Choose briggs and stratton snowblower?

The best way to choose starts with assessing your typical winter accumulation. If you regularly see more than 6 inches of snow at a time, you must go with a 2-stage blower. These units use an auger to pull snow in and an impeller to throw it out, which is much more effective than 1-stage models.

Next, follow this step-by-step selection guide:
1. Identify your engine model number located on the metal shroud.
2. Decide between a new machine or restoring your current one with a carburetor tune-up kit.
3. Prioritize electric start models to save your back and shoulders.
4. Always check for synthetic oil compatibility to ensure easy starts.
5. Avoid the mistake of buying the cheapest possible parts; stick to those with high-quality alloy construction.
6. Consider the terrain; if you have a gravel driveway, look for a model with adjustable skid shoes to prevent picking up rocks.

## FAQ

**What is the best briggs and stratton snowblower for beginners?**
For beginners, a 2-stage model with a 208cc engine and electric start is ideal. It provides enough power for most jobs without being overly complex to operate. The electric start removes the hurdle of manual pull-starting, which is often the most frustrating part for new users. Ensure you read the manual regarding fuel stabilization to keep the engine healthy.

**How much should I spend on briggs and stratton snowblower?**
A reliable residential unit typically costs between $600 and $900. If you are just buying parts to maintain an existing one, expect to spend $20 to $60 per year on oil and fuel system components. Investing in quality parts early prevents the need for a $1000 replacement later.

**What features are most important in briggs and stratton snowblower?**
The most critical features are the 2-stage clearing system, electric start, and self-propelled drive. These three features drastically reduce the physical labor involved. Additionally, having a clear LED headlight is vital for safely clearing snow during early morning hours or late winter evenings.

**How do I maintain my briggs and stratton snowblower?**
Maintenance involves changing the oil annually using 5W-30 synthetic lubricant, checking the spark plug, and ensuring the fuel system is clean. At the end of the season, either drain the gas or use a high-quality fuel stabilizer to prevent the carburetor from clogging over the summer months.

**Are expensive briggs and stratton snowblower worth it?**
Yes, the more expensive models often include features like heated grips, larger engines, and better maneuverability (like power steering). These features are well worth the investment if you have a large property or if you live in an area with frequent, heavy snowstorms where you will be using the machine often.

**What brands make the best briggs and stratton snowblower?**
While Briggs and Stratton makes the engines, brands like PowerSmart, Ariens, and Craftsman build the actual snowblower housings. These brands are highly recommended because they design their machines to integrate perfectly with Briggs engines, ensuring reliability and easy access to replacement parts.

**How long do briggs and stratton snowblower typically last?**
With proper maintenance, a Briggs and Stratton powered snowblower can easily last 20 years or more. The engines are built for durability. Most failures are due to poor fuel storage rather than mechanical breakdown, which is why keeping a spare carburetor kit on hand is a great idea.

**Can I find good briggs and stratton snowblower on a budget?**
You can find great value by looking for units with slightly smaller clearing widths (20-24 inches) or by purchasing a high-quality used unit and refurbishing it with a new $30 carburetor and fresh synthetic oil. This allows you to have a premium engine at a much lower entry price.
