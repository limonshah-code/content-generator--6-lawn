## What to Look for Before Buying the Best Indian Wall Art For Living Room

### Find Your Need - Compatibility and use case considerations
Before you start browsing, identify the vibe you want to achieve. Are you looking for a spiritual focal point like a Radha Krishna canvas, or a more subtle cultural accent like metal elephant art? Consider the current colors in your room. If your living room has neutral tones, a vibrant 5-piece canvas can provide a much-needed pop of color. If your space is already busy, a minimalist black metal piece might be a better fit to maintain balance.

### Budget - Setting realistic price expectations ($15-$150)
Wall art comes in various price tiers. Budget options ($15-$40) usually include unframed paper prints or small wooden plaques. Mid-range options ($40-$80) often feature larger single-panel canvases or detailed metalwork. Premium pieces ($80-$150+) typically involve extra-large multi-panel canvas sets or handcrafted metal plate collections. Setting a budget helps narrow down the material and size you can afford without sacrificing style.

### Key Features - Most important technical specifications
Look for features like fade-resistant inks and water-resistant materials. For metal art, ensure it has a rust-resistant coating. If you are buying prints, check if they are on canvas or paper. Canvas is generally more durable and provides a more high-end appearance. Additionally, check if the art is ready to hang or requires you to purchase separate frames, as this can add to the total cost and effort.

### Quality & Durability - What to look for in construction
High-quality wall art should feel sturdy. For canvas, look for thick material and a solid wood internal frame that won't warp over time. For metal art, ensure the edges are smooth and the coating is even. If you are buying feathers or rattan, make sure the materials are ethically sourced and naturally treated to prevent decay. Always check user reviews for mentions of longevity and whether the colors stay true over the years.

### Brand Reputation - Importance of established manufacturers
While wall art is often about the aesthetic, buying from brands like Rajukit, RITUALISTIC, or DJSYLIFE ensures a certain standard of customer service and packaging. Established brands are more likely to use environmental inks and provide sturdy packaging to prevent damage during shipping. They also tend to offer more reliable mounting hardware, which is crucial for the safety of your home.

### Portability/Size - Considerations for space and storage
Size is perhaps the most critical factor. Measure your wall space before ordering. An oversized piece can overwhelm a small room, while a tiny print can look awkward on a large blank wall. If you move frequently, consider lightweight pieces or unframed prints that are easy to pack and transport. Large framed canvases are stunning but require more care during relocation.

## What Is the Best Way to Choose indian wall art for living room?

### Step 1: Analyze Your Wall Space
Start by measuring the height and width of the area you want to fill. A good rule of thumb is that the art should cover about two-thirds to three-fourths of the available wall space.

### Step 2: Coordinate with Furniture
If the art will hang above a sofa or console table, it should be about 75% of the width of the furniture piece. This creates a cohesive look that anchors the room.

### Step 3: Select Your Medium
Decide between canvas, metal, wood, or paper. Metal is great for modern industrial looks; wood adds rustic warmth; canvas offers a classic gallery feel; and paper is the most budget-friendly for gallery walls.

### Step 4: Check Mounting Requirements
Ensure you have the right tools. Heavier pieces like metal plates or large frames may need wall anchors, while lightweight prints can often be hung with simple command strips or small nails.

### Step 5: Avoid Common Mistakes
One of the biggest mistakes is hanging art too high. The center of the piece should be at eye level (roughly 57 to 60 inches from the floor). Another mistake is choosing art that is too small for the wall, which makes the space feel empty.

### Step 6: When to Upgrade
If you have been using temporary posters, consider upgrading to a gallery-wrapped canvas or a handcrafted metal piece once you are settled in a permanent home. These upgrades provide a more professional and polished appearance.
