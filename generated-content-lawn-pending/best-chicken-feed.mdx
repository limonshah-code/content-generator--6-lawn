## What to Look for Before Buying the Best Chicken Feed

### Find Your Need
Before purchasing, identify the age and species of your birds. Laying hens require higher calcium than pullets or roosters. If you have a mixed flock of ducks and chickens, an All Flock formula is usually the safest and most convenient choice.

### Budget
Chicken feed ranges from affordable commercial blends to premium organic options. You can expect to spend anywhere from $15 to $50 per bag depending on the weight and certification. Buying in bulk sizes like 25lb or 40lb bags usually offers the best long-term value.

### Key Features
Look for protein levels between 16 percent and 18 percent for active laying hens. Essential technical additions like probiotics, Omega-3s, and pre-mixed calcium can save you the hassle of buying separate supplements later.

### Quality & Durability
Quality is found in the sourcing of the grains. Organic and non-GMO feeds ensure that you aren't passing harmful chemicals through to your eggs. Check for feeds that are milled recently to ensure the oils in the grains haven't gone rancid.

### Brand Reputation
Established manufacturers like Manna Pro or New Country Organics have strict quality control measures. Buying from reputable brands ensures consistent protein levels and mineral balances in every bag.

### Portability/Size
Consider your storage space. Large 40lb bags are economical but require a dry, pest-proof container. If you have a small backyard setup, 8lb bags are much easier to handle and keep fresh.

## Is it A Wise Decision to Buy chicken feed?

Investing in high-quality chicken feed is one of the wisest decisions a poultry keeper can make. While it might be tempting to save money with the cheapest available grain, the long-term benefits of premium nutrition far outweigh the initial cost. High-quality feed leads to healthier birds with stronger immune systems, which means fewer losses to disease and lower veterinary concerns.

Furthermore, the quality of what you put into your chickens directly impacts what you get out of them. Premium feeds result in eggs with darker, richer yolks and thicker shells that are less likely to break in the nesting box. When you compare the cost of buying high-end organic eggs at the store to the cost of producing them yourself with top-tier feed, the investment clearly pays for itself. A well-fed flock is more productive, more energetic, and more rewarding to keep.

## What Is the Best Way to Choose chicken feed?

Choosing the right feed requires a step-by-step approach to ensure your birds thrive. First, determine the life stage of your birds: starters for chicks, growers for teenagers, and layer feed for those producing eggs. Mixing these up can cause developmental issues or kidney failure.

Next, consider your own dietary preferences. If you want organic eggs, you must buy organic feed. If you have a sensitive stomach or allergies, you may want to avoid soy or corn-based feeds. Professional tip: Always keep a bag of high-protein treats like mealworms or fly larvae on hand for the molting season. Common mistakes include feeding too much scratch or kitchen scraps, which can dilute the balanced nutrition of the main feed. Always ensure the main feed makes up at least 90 percent of their total diet.

## FAQ

### What is the best chicken feed for beginners?
For most beginners, a complete layer crumble or pellet with 16 percent protein is the easiest starting point. It provides a balanced diet without requiring you to mix in extra supplements. Brands like Manna Pro offer approachable sizes and clear instructions that help new poultry owners feel confident in their flock’s nutrition.

### How much should I spend on chicken feed?
A reasonable budget is roughly $0.80 to $1.50 per pound for high-quality or organic feeds. While you can find cheaper options, they often contain fillers that result in more waste. Spending a little more on nutrient-dense feed usually means the birds eat less to satisfy their requirements, evening out the cost.

### What features are most important in chicken feed?
Protein content and calcium are the most critical features for layers. Protein supports feather and muscle health, while calcium is vital for eggshell formation. Added probiotics are also a major plus as they help maintain a healthy digestive tract, which is the bird’s first line of defense against illness.

### How do I maintain my chicken feed?
Keep your feed in a cool, dry place inside a metal or heavy-duty plastic bin with a tight-sealing lid. This prevents moisture from causing mold and keeps rodents or pests from contaminating the supply. Avoid storing feed for longer than three months to ensure the vitamins remain potent.

### Are expensive chicken feed worth it?
Yes, especially if you value organic and non-GMO standards. Expensive feeds often use whole grains and sustainable proteins rather than cheap fillers. This leads to better bird health and more nutritious eggs, which is usually the primary goal of keeping a backyard flock in the first place.

### What brands make the best chicken feed?
New Country Organics and Scratch and Peck are excellent for those seeking organic, soy-free options. Manna Pro is a highly trusted brand that offers a wide variety of formulas for mixed flocks and specific production needs, making them a reliable choice for any keeper.

### How long do chicken feed typically last?
Most dry chicken feeds have a shelf life of about 3 to 6 months if stored in optimal conditions. Grains with higher fat content can go rancid faster in hot, humid environments. It is best to buy only what you can use within a 30 to 60-day window to ensure freshness.

### Can I find good chicken feed on a budget?
Absolutely. Buying standard non-organic pellets in bulk (25lb to 50lb bags) is the best way to save money. You can still provide excellent nutrition by choosing a reputable brand's standard line and supplementing with free-ranging or high-quality kitchen scraps to round out their diet.
