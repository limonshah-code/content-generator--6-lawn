## What to Look for Before Buying the Best Lawn Mower Blades For Scag

### 1. Find Your Need - Compatibility and use case considerations
Before purchasing, you must identify your deck size. Scag mowers commonly come in 36, 48, 52, 61, and 72-inch configurations. A 52-inch deck usually requires three 18-inch blades, while a 61-inch deck requires three 21-inch blades. Using the wrong size can cause deck damage or a poor cut. Consider your grass type as well; thick, wet grass requires high-lift blades, while dry, thin grass might be better suited for mulching blades.

### 2. Budget - Setting realistic price expectations ($30-$150)
Replacement blades can range from $30 for a basic 3-pack of aftermarket blades to over $150 for a 6-pack of high-end OEM or specialized mulching blades. If you are a homeowner, a mid-range set often suffices. Professionals should consider bulk packs (6-packs) from reputable brands like Oregon to reduce the cost per blade, as they will go through sets much faster.

### 3. Key Features - Most important technical specifications
Look at the 'lift' of the blade. High-lift blades (1-inch or more) are great for discharging and bagging. Medium-lift or mulching blades (3/4-inch) are better for returning nutrients to the soil. Also, check the center hole size. Most Scag mowers use a 5/8-inch round center hole, but always verify with your manual. Thickness is another factor; 0.250-inch blades are much more durable than the standard 0.203-inch.

### 4. Quality & Durability - What to look for in construction
Blades made from hardened steel or specialized alloys like Marbain are the most durable. Check if the blade is powder-coated or spray-coated to prevent rust. A good blade should be resistant to bending and should hold an edge for at least 20-30 hours of mowing time. Look for 'heat-treated' or 'quenched' in the product description, as this indicates a more robust manufacturing process.

### 5. Brand Reputation - Importance of established manufacturers
Scag Genuine OEM parts are the gold standard for fit and balance. However, brands like Oregon and USA Mower Blade have established themselves as reliable aftermarket alternatives that often offer better value. Avoid generic, unbranded blades that don't list specific part number compatibility, as they can be poorly balanced and damage your mower's spindles.

### 6. Portability/Size - Considerations for space and storage
While 'portability' isn't usually a factor for the mower itself, storing spare blades is. If you buy bulk 6-packs, ensure you have a dry place to store them to prevent rust. Keeping a spare set on your service trailer or in your garage ensures that a dull or broken blade doesn't stop your work day.
