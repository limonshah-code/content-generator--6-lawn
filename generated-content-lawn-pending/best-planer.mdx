## What to Look for Before Buying the Best Planer

### Find Your Need - Compatibility and use case considerations
Before purchasing, determine if you need a thickness planer or a hand planer. A thickness planer like the DEWALT DW735X is designed to make the two faces of a board parallel and achieve a specific thickness. A hand planer is better for jobsite trimming, smoothing edges, or tapering shims. If you process rough lumber, a benchtop thicknesser is essential. If you primarily do install work, go cordless.

### Budget - Setting realistic price expectations ($150-$700 ranges)
Hand planers typically range from $50 to $200. Entry-level benchtop planers usually start around $300 (like the WEN or Craftsman models), while high-end professional benchtop units can reach $600 to $800. Investing more usually gets you better snipe prevention, more knives, and more powerful dust collection systems.

### Key Features - Most important technical specifications
Look for motor amperage (15 Amps is standard for benchtop models) and cutterhead speed (RPM). The number of knives also matters; three knives generally produce a smoother finish than two. Another critical feature is a carriage lock, which keeps the cutterhead from moving as wood enters or exits, preventing the dreaded 'snipe' (a deeper cut at the end of the board).

### Quality & Durability - What to look for in construction
Cast aluminum or cast iron bases are significantly more stable and durable than stamped steel. Check the quality of the infeed and outfeed tables. If they are flimsy, they will flex under the weight of long boards, leading to inaccurate cuts. High carbon steel or carbide-tipped blades will also last much longer than standard steel.

### Brand Reputation - Importance of established manufacturers
Brands like DEWALT, WEN, and CRAFTSMAN have long histories in the tool industry. DEWALT is known for professional-grade durability, while WEN offers excellent value and innovation in the hobbyist space. Choosing an established brand ensures that replacement blades and parts will be available for years to come.

### Portability/Size - Considerations for space and storage
Benchtop planers are heavy, often weighing between 60 and 90 pounds. If you don't have a dedicated space, look for models with built-in handles or those that can be easily mounted to a mobile tool stand. If you have a tiny shop, a 12.5-inch model might be easier to store than a 13-inch heavy-duty unit.

## Is it A Wise Decision to Buy planer?

Investing in a planer is one of the smartest decisions a woodworker can make because it pays for itself by allowing you to buy rough-sawn lumber. Rough lumber is significantly cheaper than S4S (surfaced on four sides) wood found at big-box retailers. By surfacing your own wood, you gain complete control over the final thickness of your projects, which is vital for high-quality joinery.

Furthermore, a planer allows you to reclaim old wood. You can take gray, weathered fence boards or pallet wood and transform them into beautiful, clean timber in a single pass. While the initial cost of $300-$600 might seem high, the long-term savings on material costs and the ability to produce professional-grade finishes make it an indispensable tool for anyone serious about woodworking.

## What Is the Best Way to Choose planer?

1.  **Assess Your Frequency of Use:** If you plan on running hundreds of board feet every month, invest in a three-knife or spiral-head model like the DW735X for longevity.
2.  **Check Your Power Supply:** Ensure your workshop can handle a 15-amp tool, as planers draw significant power, especially when starting up or cutting wide hardwoods.
3.  **Evaluate Your Dust Collection:** Planers create more wood chips than almost any other tool. If you don't have a high-powered vacuum, look for a model with fan-assisted ejection.
4.  **Prioritize Snipe Prevention:** Look for machines with carriage locks or very long, adjustable support tables.
5.  **Consider Blade Maintenance:** If you hate sharpening or alignment, a spiral cutterhead with rotatable inserts is a much better choice than traditional straight knives.
6.  **Read Recent Reviews:** Look for feedback regarding customer service and the ease of sourcing replacement parts for the specific model you are considering.

## FAQ

**What is the best planer for beginners?**
The WEN PL1252 or the CRAFTSMAN CMEW320 are excellent for beginners. They are simple to operate, relatively affordable, and provide enough power to learn the basics of stock preparation without a massive financial commitment.

**How much should I spend on planer?**
For a hobbyist, $300 to $450 is the sweet spot for a reliable benchtop machine. Professionals or those working with very hard woods should expect to spend $600 or more for advanced features like dual speeds and spiral heads.

**What features are most important in planer?**
A powerful 15-amp motor, a carriage lock to prevent snipe, and efficient dust collection are the top three features. A material removal gauge is also very helpful for preventing motor strain.

**How do I maintain my planer?**
Regularly clean the feed rollers with mineral spirits to prevent slipping. Keep the tables waxed for a smooth glide, and always check your knives for nicks. If using a spiral head, ensure the inserts are torqued correctly.

**Are expensive planer worth it?**
Yes, because more expensive models usually offer better snipe control and higher cuts-per-inch. This means you spend much less time sanding your boards after they have been planed.

**What brands make the best planer?**
DEWALT is currently the leader for benchtop units. WEN is the top choice for value and spiral technology at lower prices, while Makita and Bosch make excellent handheld options.

**How long do planer typically last?**
A well-maintained benchtop planer can easily last 10-15 years in a hobbyist shop. Hand planers can last even longer if the brushes and belts are replaced periodically.

**Can I find good planer on a budget?**
Absolutely. Brands like WEN have revolutionized the market by providing high-capacity machines with 15-amp motors for under $300, which are perfect for home workshops.
